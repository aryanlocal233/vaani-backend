Provider risk analysis — decision support, not a decision

Vaani depends entirely on one API. Here's what could replace it, and what actually would.

Sarvam AI failing (outage, rate limit, pricing change, deprecation) currently stops the whole app — STT, translation, and TTS all go through one vendor with no fallback. This compares real alternatives on cost, language coverage, and whether they actually remove the single-point-of-failure risk or just relocate it.

What each option actually solves

Bhashini — cheapest fast-follow, and you've already half-built it

This exact codebase has dormant Bhashini scaffolding (main.py, config.py) from before the Sarvam pivot — wiring it back in as a fallback is meaningfully less work than integrating a new vendor from scratch. Government-backed, likely free or near-free for a mela-authority deployment specifically, and covers Odia by design (it's a national-mission platform, not an afterthought). The tradeoff: government infrastructure has historically been less consistent on uptime/latency than commercial clouds — worth validating with your own load test before trusting it as the primary fallback, not just assuming.

Azure — cheapest commercial cloud, but reshapes where your cost goes

Azure's translate (₹0.00085/char) and TTS (₹0.0014/char) are both cheaper than Sarvam's — but its STT (₹1.42/min) is nearly 3× pricier. Since caching already minimizes repeat translate/TTS cost, STT is what scales with raw conversation count regardless of caching — meaning at a mature, heavily-cached deployment, switching entirely to Azure could net out more expensive, not less, even though two of three line items look cheaper in isolation. Best used as a fallback for when Sarvam is down, not a wholesale replacement.

AWS — ruled out as a full replacement

Amazon Polly's Indian-language TTS coverage is Hindi and Indian English only. A Bengali or Odia pilgrim would hit the exact same silent-wrong-language failure class already fixed once this session. Not viable as a single fallback provider without pairing it with a different TTS vendor for the other 10 languages — at which point you've built two integrations for the price of one.

AI4Bharat (self-hosted) — the only option that removes the risk category, not just the vendor

Every other option here is still "a different company's API can rate-limit or go down." A self-hosted open-source stack (IndicConformer + IndicTrans2 + Indic-Parler-TTS, all MIT-licensed) is the only path that actually eliminates external-API dependency for the core pipeline — no rate limits, no per-request billing, no third-party outage risk, and it covers languages Sarvam currently gates behind its own beta approval (Maithili, Urdu, and others already blocked in your testing). The real cost is ops: you'd be running and maintaining GPU inference yourselves for the first time, which is a genuine new responsibility, not a config change.

The distinction worth deciding on first

"Add a second API" and "stop depending on any single API" are different goals, and the right next step depends on which one you actually want:

  • If the goal is "survive a Sarvam outage/rate-limit without the app going dark": wire in Bhashini (cheapest, partially built already) or Azure as an automatic fallback behind a provider-abstraction layer — a circuit breaker that switches over on repeated failures. Still ultimately dependent on some external API, just not only one.
  • If the goal is "never depend on any external vendor for the core pipeline again": that's only AI4Bharat, self-hosted — a bigger, different project (standing up and maintaining GPU inference), not a config swap.
