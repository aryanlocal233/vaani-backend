How auto-selection works

For each request, only providers that are configured (real credentials) and support the language needed are candidates. If none of that capability's providers are forced below, candidates are ranked cheapest-first by an effective cost:

effective_cost = raw_price × (1 + 2×recent_failure_rate + 1×recent_quality_flag_rate)

A provider that's been failing or producing suspect output recently (last 20 calls) gets pushed down the ranking even if its list price is lowest — its real-world cost is higher than the sticker price once retries and bad answers are counted. A provider is skipped entirely while its circuit breaker is open (3 consecutive failures, or over 50% of its last 10 calls failed) — it gets a 30s cooldown before being tried again, doubling each time a retry fails, up to 5 minutes, and resetting the moment it succeeds again.

If the top candidate's call fails anyway, the next candidate is tried immediately, within the same request — not just "better luck next time." Every call, whichever provider handled it, is logged below with its real cost and outcome.

Force-selecting a provider below overrides all of this — that provider alone is used, with no automatic failover, for deliberate testing/control.
